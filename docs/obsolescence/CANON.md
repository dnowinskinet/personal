# Obsolescence, Inc. — Design Canon

Reconciled from R1–R9. This is what to build against.

**Status:** produced by the Phase 2 synthesis, 2026-07-26. Supersedes any single report where they
disagree — see [CONTRADICTIONS.md](CONTRADICTIONS.md) for what disagreed and why each resolution was
chosen.

**How to read a citation.** `[R1,R5]` means those reports independently support the claim. More
reports is stronger evidence, with one caveat: several reports inherited framing from earlier ones
through their briefs rather than reading them, so agreement is not always independence. Where that
matters it is stated.

**What this document is not.** It is not a spec. Numbers marked *provisional* are working
assumptions that a named gate will confirm or overturn. Anything genuinely undecided lives in
[OPEN-QUESTIONS.md](OPEN-QUESTIONS.md), not here.

## 1. The loop

**One quarter is one release cycle.** [R1,R3,R4,R5,R6,R9 — six reports, the strongest agreement in
the corpus] R1 derived it comparatively against five rejected cadences; everything downstream
assumes it.

**Every quarter ends in a public lineup event, never a modal score screen.** [R1,R3,R5,R6,R9] R1's
inherited rule from *News Tower* is the load-bearing sentence: *every cycle must end in a public
object whose omissions hurt*. The equivalent of column inches is finite launch bandwidth — showroom
space, keynote time, demo stations, production setup, marketing attention, retailer slots, support
readiness.

**The recurring question**, which must stay fertile across every cycle: *what do we launch now, what
do we delay, what do we keep alive, and what future pain are we buying to make today's launch look
coherent?*

**Four phases per quarter** [R1, elaborated by R4's Golden Quarter]: concept and triage → prototype
and promise → lock and load → launch and aftershock. The lock milestone is the point of no return;
late changes after it cost cross-department penalties.

**Quarter length: 25–35 minutes of active play.** [R1,R4,R5,R9] *Provisional.* R1 contradicts itself
— its formal handoff says 25–35 while its experiential prose says 20–40. The handoff figure wins
because four reports built on it. Confirmed or overturned at Gate B.

**Launch day: 7–9 real-time minutes inside the quarter, 4–6 strategic interventions.** [R5,R9 —
independently] Pausable. 1× and 2× during launch theater; 4× discouraged. The player never clicks
every sale or answers every question.

**Pause is a first-class design tool.** [R1,R5,R8] Free pause any time, speeds 1×/2×/4×, optional
auto-pause on red alerts, optional phase-gate pauses at lock deadlines. Three alert severities: FYI,
decision soon, decision now. Anything below *decision soon* must be batchable or auto-resolvable by
policy.

**Failure wounds, it does not end the run.** [R1,R5,R6,R7] A bad quarter creates inherited pain,
debt, and recovery opportunities. Hard failure is campaign-level only.

## 2. The lineup

**Three simultaneously sellable offers plus one ecosystem slot.** [R1,R2,R5,R3,R9] Typically only
two are newly designed each quarter; the low end is normally a **carried-over prior-generation
variant that remains sellable** — with its own price, stock allocation, display placement and
lifecycle status — not merely an installed-base cohort.

This resolves the loudest product-model conflict in the corpus. R1 said four slots with a carry-over
low end; R2 described three freshly-designed variants per generation; R4's slice used two new plus
one inherited. All three are compatible under the ruling above, and it matters because it decides
whether an `Offer` entity exists at all.

**A product is a family: shared platform + variant promises + lifecycle obligations.** [R1,R2,R4,R5,R6]
Never a bundle of stats. Support promises, repair stance, compatibility promises and trade-in rules
are part of the product, not aftercare.

**First category: a fictional smartphone/handheld platform.** [R2,R3,R4,R5,R6,R7,R9] A composite
fiction, never a recognisable parody of one real brand — R7 supplies the mitigation R2's approach
requires.

**Battery architecture is the canonical worked example** of one decision propagating across the
whole lifecycle [R3,R4,R8,R9], and **the connector/compatibility standard is the exemplar
cross-cutting content object** [R1,R2,R4,R5,R6,R7,R9]. Both earn first-class status.

**Partial component sharing is deliberate design material.** [R2,R4,R5,R6,R7] Defect scope must span
component / variant / platform / generation so one supplier fault can hit part of a lineup but not
all of it.

**Second category is post-release.** [R2,R3,R4,R5,R6,R9] Printers and home-office devices are the
designated extension stress test [R2,R3,R5] — R2 scored them into an exact 52–52 tie with
smartphones and demoted them on showroom glamour alone.

## 3. Memory and compression

**Older generations compress into aggregated cohorts, never per-unit simulation.** [R1,R2,R5,R6,R8,R9
— the most widely supported systemic claim in the corpus]

**Compression is a property of the installed base and of retired SKUs, never of the family as a
whole.** A family can be simultaneously compressed — its installed base is cohorts — *and*
per-offer visible, because one of its SKUs still occupies a live lineup slot. This is the resolution
of a blocking conflict between R9's per-offer rule and R6's family-level rule, and it is what makes
the carried-over low end coherent.

**No more than three individually foregrounded generations.** [R1,R4,R5,R6,R9] No report proposes
more. R1 names over-persistence as a kill criterion. *Provisional:* R1 says two, R6 says three
(current, previous, burden), R9 says current + previous with the burden family surfacing only when
decision-relevant. R9's rule is adopted — it is the same cap expressed as a display default.

**Compressed cohorts resurface only through named, decision-relevant hooks.** [R1,R2,R5,R6,R9]
Support sunset, defect, trade-in, repair, incompatibility, institutional contract, regulation.
Resurfacing promotes a cohort to a temporary burden card; it never re-expands the full SKU tree.

**Retirement never deletes history.** [R2,R5,R6] Installed base, accessory adoption, repair demand,
support promises and reputation residue all survive.

**Persistent state must be mechanically load-bearing.** [R2,R5,R6,R9] It persists only if it can
create a new decision, a visible consequence, or an ending condition.

## 4. Campaign

**15 quarters across 5 chapters.** *Provisional, adopted over R6's 18/6.* R9 argued 15 and demoted
18 as an unproven hypothesis with "very high" cost if wrong — but **R9 never read R6**, so this is
not a case of the later report knowing better. It is adopted because the production risk is
asymmetric: 18 quarters costs more content in every category and R6's own argument for 18 rests on
its chapter *rhythm*, which survives intact at 15. Hold 18/6 as a post-Gate-H stretch, 12/4 as the
rung down.

**Keep R6's Foundation → Market → Liability three-quarter chapter rhythm.** That is the part of R6
that is genuinely argued: Foundation asks what we are building and promising, Market asks what
happens when we ask people to buy it, Liability asks what survives contact with ownership.

**The Liability quarter must create opportunity, not just penalty.** [R6,R7] Profitable
refurbishment, trust recovery, support differentiation, learning from returns, repair capacity as
competitive advantage, or deliberately abandoning an obligation to free capital. If it reads as
"clear the backlog before the real fun resumes", the structure has failed.

**Finishable and single-category, with a formal ending then optional continued play.** [R1,R2,R5,R6,R7,R9]
Not an endless sandbox, not a roguelite that discards history.

**Three viable doctrines, not morality classes:** durable/open/service-heavy, premium/integrated,
scale-first/volume. [R6,R7] Each is differently burdened rather than differently virtuous. If one
wins profit *and* trust *and* resilience simultaneously, it is dominant and needs rebalancing.

⚠ **The ending set is unresolved.** R6 and R7 ship two different sets of five endings and only two
map cleanly. See [CONTRADICTIONS.md](CONTRADICTIONS.md) C-11.

## 5. The building

**Fixed-width vertical section, fit-to-width by default, no page-level horizontal scrolling.**
[R3,R4,R8,R9] Vertical movement scrolls floors, not the browser page. Horizontal pan unlocks only
after zooming past the default fit.

**Three shallow depth lanes** per ordinary floor: front (public reading), middle (primary
interaction), rear (service and buffer). [R3]

**Street-level double-height showroom hinge; B1 dock/ramp industrial hinge.** [R3] Configurable
project bands above, modular lifecycle bands below. Separate personnel and freight cores — visibly
related, never mechanically merged [R3,R4,R8]. Customers never use the building's vertical
transport.

**Canonical gray-box: six floors.** One project floor, the double-height showroom, B1 dock/ramp, one
prototype/test floor, one merged pilot/production floor, one merged diagnostics/repair floor
carrying a quarantine branch. This resolves a blocking conflict in which R3 and R8 each gave two
mutually incompatible answers.

**Viewport framing:** ~1024×640 shows roughly 4–5 ordinary floors; a phone at ~390×844 shows 1–2 and
**never** the whole tower. [R3,R8] Note: this is *not* independent convergence — R3 states it
inherited R8's fixed-width, fit-to-width and aggregation framing through its brief. Treat it as one
source, not two.

**Mature first campaign ≈16 levels; stress 24–32; renderer must support 32 without restart.** [R3,R8]

**Phone is a different composition, not a scaled desktop.** [R1,R3,R4,R5,R8,R9] One floor or one
double-height anchor, a persistent floor rail, bottom sheets rather than hover. Any primary action
within about three taps. A short phone session supports exactly three high-value actions: respond to
an urgent alert, change one live priority, inspect readiness or one outcome [R1,R5].

**All entity budgets are readability budgets, not technical maxima.** [R3,R4,R5,R8] The stated
failure mode is an unreadable crowd, not a dropped frame.

## 6. Operations

**Modular bays in a fixed macro-structure.** [R3,R4,R8,R9] Fixed service trunks, inbound and outbound
spines, shared elevator capacity. Bays occupy 1–3 adjacent sockets and hold 2–4 internal station
packages.

**The player never draws belts, corridors, conveyors, floorplans or per-item routes.**
[R1,R3,R4,R5,R8,R9 — six reports] Routing between bays is automatic along the canonical chain. The
player's verbs are package composition, staffing, product assignment, reserve rules, priorities,
retooling and policy.

**Canonical chain:** opportunity → concept/architecture → prototype → pilot → production → launch
inventory → sales/claims → returns → diagnostics → repair → refurbishment → recycle/dispose →
next-quarter inheritance. [R4]

**Reverse logistics is real, not a refund number.** [R4,R3] B1 splits into four branches: production
stock, protected service reserve, reverse-flow intake, and quarantine. Returns compete with forward
production for labour, space and parts — that competition is what makes obsolescence operational
rather than thematic.

**Automation removes repetition and creates policy decisions.** [R1,R4,R6] Maximum automation must
not be universally optimal: it should introduce brittleness, forecast dependence, slower edge-case
response and higher switching cost.

**Suppliers are a few critical-component contracts, not a sourcing spreadsheet.** [R2,R4,R9 —
R2 and R4 converged independently; R4 could not see R2.]

## 7. Market and judgment

**Cohorts determine scale; visible agents reveal causes.** [R1,R2,R3,R5,R6,R8] The market is
`segment × channel × installed-base-state` cohorts computed from the same rules as visible
individuals. Visible customers are **representative evidence, not tiny gods whose purchases are
multiplied into global sales**.

**The flagship showroom is an observatory, not the market.** [R1,R3,R5] Its literal sales are a
modest share of volume. It influences the wider market only through bounded, explicitly named
effects — direct sales, demonstration credibility, press attention, launch-story formation,
learning.

**Four showroom states on one unchanging footprint:** ordinary operation, launch preparation, launch
theater, aftermath/service recovery. [R3,R5] Achieved through lighting, signage, barriers, staff
positions and stage hardware — never rebuilding.

**Three simultaneously comparable product tables** (old/low, Standard, Premium) around a shared
comparison surface, with no forced circulation route. [R3,R5]

**Seven conflict-bearing customer segments**, each carrying at least one **active dislike or veto**.
[R2,R5] R2's and R5's lists are the same set renamed — R5 says so explicitly. Value Planners,
Mainstream Upgraders, Prestige/Novelty Seekers, Capability Enthusiasts, Long-Horizon Owners,
Control/Privacy Guardians, Institutional Operators. Accessibility, urgency, budget and device
condition are **cross-cutting constraints, not identities**.

**Choice pipeline:** trigger → hard must-have veto → consideration set → inspection/demo →
confidence → friction → outcome → later satisfaction/support/return. [R5] Hard constraints veto
*before* any soft scoring. R5's structure supersedes R2's simpler likes/dislikes scoring.

**No single review score and no overall grade.** [R1,R5,R6,R7] Five dimensions permitted — expected —
to disagree: reach, portfolio performance, lineup clarity, promise and trust, ownership health. A
quarter can be commercially excellent and lifecycle-dangerous.

**Delay, backorder, substitution, accessory-only purchase, competitor purchase, repair and
no-purchase are all first-class outcomes**, not failure states. [R1,R2,R5]

**Every reported market effect must cite a source event and resolve through a visible cause chain.**
[R1,R2,R4,R5,R7] Generic hidden modifiers are forbidden — "showroom prestige +8% global demand" is
explicitly named as the anti-pattern.

**No demographic inference.** [R5,R7] A customer's appearance never determines their needs.

## 8. Tone

**Satirize institutional incentives, not "products designed to fail".** [R7] The primary thesis is
that sensible local decisions about segmentation, speed, elegance, support, compatibility and growth
compound into a system where replacement feels inevitable and responsibility is diffused.

**The player is the company's operating logic** — an ongoing executive committee embodied as the
institution — not a heroic founder and not a single department head. [R7]

**Complicity emerges through attractive local problem-solving.** [R7] The UI tells the truth about
the immediate upside. Three beats: justification → residue → secondary adaptation. The workaround
should itself be funny and compromising.

**Customers are never the joke.** [R1,R5,R7] Nor are workers, repair techs, low-income buyers, or
affected communities. Targets are segmentation rituals, executive euphemism, incompatible promises,
manufactured scarcity, metric-chasing, and the player's own optimizing instinct.

**Register split:** executives speak in abstractions (*portfolio coherence, support optimization,
certified circularity*); workers and customers speak in concrete nouns (*the bench is full, the
battery swelled, the old charger doesn't fit*). R7 calls this "one of the safest and funniest
engines in the whole game".

**Bright surfaces, dark logistics.** Keep the world attractive; darkness belongs in consequences and
backstage spaces. Sincerity at the point of harm — once a subject crosses into injury or coercion,
the joke travels upward toward policy, never downward toward people.

⚠ **R7's worker-humanity pillar has no mechanism.** R4, R6 and R8 all model workers as pooled
abstractions, and R5's representative-evidence apparatus covers customers only. See C-14.

## 9. Technical

Enforced constraints live in [INVARIANTS.md](INVARIANTS.md); the architecture is in
[ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md). Canon-level agreements:

**Static, client-only browser game.** [R5,R6,R8,R9] No backend, accounts, multiplayer or runtime LLM
calls. Local-first persistence, GitHub Pages first.

**Pure seeded command-driven deterministic core; the renderer is a projection.** [R5,R6,R8,R9] No
renderer object may be the source of truth for choice, stock, trust, queue or ownership state.

**Determinism is per-journey, not merely per-aggregate.** R5's stronger invariant is adopted over
R8's "same high-level outcome": seed + command log must reproduce identical per-agent journeys and
cohort outcomes. This is a blocking resolution — it constrains numeric representation and PRNG
architecture, so it had to be settled before the kernel was built. It is already reflected in
I-004.

**Mechanics before art, without exception.** [R1,R3,R4,R5,R8,R9] The first artifact is a headless or
gray-box falsifier of decisions, never a visual slice.

**Angular DOM owns text-heavy, explanatory and accessible UI; the canvas owns the world.** [R3,R4,R5,R8]

**Aggregate rather than simulate individually.** [R1,R2,R3,R4,R5,R6,R8 — seven reports] Cohorts not
per-unit devices, batches not per-item routing, crowds aggregated beyond near-camera detail.

**Phaser 4 + Angular shell is the starting stack; PixiJS 8.x is the single named fallback**, decided
by running a gauntlet on a real loadout, never on paper. [R8,R9 — **ordering reversed by D-033**,
2026-07-31; R8 and R9 named Pixi first, and later research argued the gate needs a game framework
rather than a renderer. The method survives; the stack swapped.]

**Renderer-agnostic work comes first.** Lazy route, sim-core with fixed tick and command log,
save/load and migration harness, developer lab and device matrix — none of which touch the renderer.
The camera and floor-band code waits for the Gate C bake-off, because writing it against an
undecided renderer means discarding or porting it.

**Content is data-authored with immutable IDs**; old definitions are never deleted from saves;
removed content becomes an inspectable tombstone that still carries obligations. [R2,R6,R8,R9]

**AI-agent extensibility is an acceptance criterion.** [R8,R9] Adding one new floor type and one new
incident must be registry/data/prefab work with no renderer-core or sim-kernel surgery.
